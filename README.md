# 100 Unsolved Math Problems

An open, curated catalogue of **100 of the most famous open problems in mathematics** — and a place where anyone can propose ideas, partial results, and *hypothetical* approaches toward solving them.

> ⭐ If you find this useful or fun, star the repo. Contributions welcome — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

---

## What this is (and isn't)

**This is:**
- A well-referenced reference for each problem: precise statement, history, current state of the art, and links.
- A community space to share *approaches* — heuristics, reformulations, computational evidence, partial results, or "here's an angle nobody seems to have tried."

**This is not:**
- A place to claim you've solved the Riemann Hypothesis in a `.txt` file. 😄

Please read this before contributing a "solution":

> **The crackpot filter.** Famous open problems attract thousands of flawed "proofs" every year (the Collatz conjecture alone is legendary for this). A real proof of any problem here would be a historic event, published in a peer-reviewed journal and verified by experts — not merged into a GitHub repo. **That does not mean your ideas are worthless.** Reformulations, connections between problems, computational experiments, and honest partial progress are genuinely valuable and welcome. We just ask you to be **rigorous and humble**: state your assumptions, show your work, and clearly mark what is proven vs. conjectured. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for the required format.

> ⚠️ **Status disclaimer.** Each problem is marked *Open* to the best of our knowledge **as of the repository's creation (2026)**. Mathematics moves — some problems on this list may be resolved after that date, and edge cases are debated. If you know a problem here has been solved or disproven, **please open a PR** with a citation. This catalogue is only as good as its community keeps it.

`★M` = one of the six still-open [Clay Millennium Prize Problems](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/) ($1,000,000 each). (The seventh, the Poincaré conjecture, was solved by Grigori Perelman in 2003.)

## The List

### Number Theory & Prime Numbers
1. **Riemann Hypothesis** `★M` — all non-trivial zeros of ζ(s) have real part ½.
2. **Goldbach's Conjecture** — every even integer > 2 is a sum of two primes.
3. **Twin Prime Conjecture** — there are infinitely many primes p with p+2 also prime.
4. **Collatz Conjecture (3n+1)** — the 3n+1 iteration always reaches 1.
5. **Legendre's Conjecture** — there is always a prime between n² and (n+1)².
6. **Landau's n²+1 Problem** — are there infinitely many primes of the form n²+1?
7. **Brocard's Problem** — for which n is n!+1 a perfect square (Brown numbers)?
8. **Odd Perfect Numbers** — does an odd perfect number exist?
9. **Infinitude of Mersenne Primes** — are there infinitely many primes 2^p−1?
10. **Fermat Primes** — are there finitely or infinitely many primes 2^(2^n)+1?
11. **abc Conjecture** — bounds rad(abc) for coprime a+b=c (Mochizuki's claimed proof remains unaccepted by the wider community).
12. **Beal Conjecture** — if aˣ+bʸ=cᶻ with x,y,z>2 then a,b,c share a common factor.
13. **Fermat–Catalan Conjecture** — finitely many coprime solutions to aᵐ+bⁿ=cᵏ with 1/m+1/n+1/k<1.
14. **Erdős–Straus Conjecture** — 4/n = 1/x+1/y+1/z solvable for all n>1.
15. **Lehmer's Totient Problem** — does φ(n) | (n−1) imply n is prime?
16. **Carmichael's Totient Conjecture** — no φ-value is attained exactly once.
17. **Gilbreath's Conjecture** — iterated absolute differences of primes always start with 1.
18. **Polignac's Conjecture** — every even number is the gap between infinitely many consecutive prime pairs.
19. **Singmaster's Conjecture** — a uniform bound on how often a number >1 appears in Pascal's triangle.
20. **Cramér's Conjecture** — prime gaps are O((log p)²).
21. **Firoozbakht's Conjecture** — pₙ^(1/n) is strictly decreasing.
22. **Bunyakovsky Conjecture** — irreducible integer polynomials produce infinitely many primes (under mild conditions).
23. **Schinzel's Hypothesis H** — a joint generalization of many prime-producing conjectures.
24. **Grimm's Conjecture** — distinct prime factors can be assigned to consecutive composite numbers.
25. **Giuga's Conjecture** — a number-theoretic characterization of primes via a power-sum congruence.

### Analytic Number Theory & L-functions
26. **Generalized Riemann Hypothesis** — RH for all Dirichlet L-functions.
27. **Lindelöf Hypothesis** — ζ(½+it) grows slower than any positive power of t.
28. **Chowla Conjecture** — the Liouville function has no correlations.
29. **Elliott–Halberstam Conjecture** — strong averaged control of primes in arithmetic progressions.
30. **Montgomery's Pair Correlation Conjecture** — spacing of ζ zeros matches random matrix theory.
31. **Hardy–Littlewood k-Tuple Conjecture** — asymptotic density of prime constellations.
32. **Second Hardy–Littlewood Conjecture** — π(x+y) ≤ π(x)+π(y) (believed to conflict with the k-tuple conjecture).
33. **Grand Riemann Hypothesis** — RH for all automorphic L-functions.

### Diophantine & Algebraic Number Theory
34. **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture** `★M` — rank of an elliptic curve equals the order of vanishing of its L-function.
35. **Hall's Conjecture** — lower bounds on |x³−y²|.
36. **Littlewood Conjecture** — simultaneous Diophantine approximation of two reals.
37. **Vojta's Conjecture** — a deep height inequality unifying much of Diophantine geometry.
38. **Bombieri–Lang Conjecture** — distribution of rational points on varieties of general type.
39. **Hilbert's Twelfth Problem** — explicit generation of abelian extensions of number fields.
40. **Zaremba's Conjecture** — every denominator admits a continued fraction with bounded partial quotients.

### Combinatorics
41. **Union-Closed Sets Conjecture (Frankl)** — some element lies in ≥ half the sets of any union-closed family.
42. **Sunflower Conjecture** — bounds on set systems forced to contain sunflowers.
43. **Hadamard Matrix Conjecture** — a Hadamard matrix exists for every order divisible by 4.
44. **1/3–2/3 Conjecture** — every finite non-total poset has a pair comparable in [1/3, 2/3] of linear extensions.
45. **Erdős–Turán Conjecture on Additive Bases** — additive bases of order 2 have unbounded representation function.
46. **Erdős Conjecture on Arithmetic Progressions** — sets with divergent reciprocal sums contain arbitrarily long APs.
47. **Ramsey Number R(5,5)** — determine its exact value (known to lie between 43 and 48).
48. **Lonely Runner Conjecture** — every runner gets "lonely" on a circular track.
49. **Caccetta–Häggkvist Conjecture** — short cycles forced by minimum out-degree in digraphs.
50. **Tuza's Conjecture** — triangle edge-cover vs. triangle packing ratio.
51. **Rota's Basis Conjecture** — rearranging n bases into n disjoint transversal bases.
52. **Erdős–Gyárfás Conjecture** — every cubic graph has a cycle of length a power of 2.

### Graph Theory
53. **Hadwiger Conjecture** — a k-chromatic graph has a K_k minor.
54. **Reconstruction Conjecture (Ulam)** — graphs are determined by their vertex-deleted subgraphs.
55. **Graceful Tree Conjecture (Ringel–Kotzig)** — every tree has a graceful labeling.
56. **Cycle Double Cover Conjecture** — every bridgeless graph has a family of cycles covering each edge twice.
57. **Total Coloring Conjecture** — total chromatic number is at most Δ+2.
58. **Berge–Fulkerson Conjecture** — every bridgeless cubic graph has 6 perfect matchings covering each edge twice.
59. **Oberwolfach Problem** — 2-factorizations of complete graphs into prescribed cycles.
60. **Barnette's Conjecture** — every bipartite cubic planar 3-connected graph is Hamiltonian.

### Discrete & Combinatorial Geometry
61. **Inscribed Square Problem (Toeplitz)** — every Jordan curve contains 4 points forming a square.
62. **Kissing Number Problem** — maximum touching unit spheres in dimensions beyond 1–4, 8, 24.
63. **Sphere Packing** — optimal packing density in dimensions other than 1, 2, 3, 8, 24.
64. **Hadwiger–Nelson Problem** — chromatic number of the plane (known to be 5, 6, or 7).
65. **Kobon Triangle Problem** — maximum triangles formed by n lines.
66. **Erdős Unit Distance Problem** — maximum unit distances among n points in the plane.
67. **Ulam's Packing Conjecture** — the ball is the worst-packing convex body in 3D.
68. **Reinhardt Conjecture** — the smoothed octagon is the worst-packing convex body in 2D.
69. **Borsuk's Problem** — dimensions in which every bounded set splits into d+1 smaller-diameter pieces.
70. **Happy Ending Problem** — points forcing a convex n-gon (Erdős–Szekeres exact bound).
71. **Moser's Worm Problem** — smallest region covering every unit-length curve.
72. **Danzer's Problem** — does a bounded-density set meeting every convex body of volume 1 exist?

### Topology & Geometric Topology
73. **Smooth 4-Dimensional Poincaré Conjecture** — is every homotopy 4-sphere diffeomorphic to S⁴?
74. **Novikov Conjecture** — homotopy invariance of higher signatures.
75. **Baum–Connes Conjecture** — K-theory of group C*-algebras.
76. **Zeeman Conjecture** — collapsibility of certain contractible 2-complexes.
77. **Andrews–Curtis Conjecture** — trivializing balanced presentations of the trivial group.
78. **Hilbert–Smith Conjecture** — only Lie groups act faithfully on manifolds.
79. **Volume Conjecture** — colored Jones polynomials recover hyperbolic volume of knots.

### Algebra, Groups & Rings
80. **Kaplansky's Zero-Divisor Conjecture** — group rings of torsion-free groups have no zero divisors.
81. **Jacobian Conjecture** — polynomial maps with constant nonzero Jacobian are invertible.
82. **Dixmier Conjecture** — endomorphisms of the Weyl algebra are automorphisms.
83. **Inverse Galois Problem** — is every finite group a Galois group over ℚ?
84. **Zariski Cancellation Problem** — does Aⁿ ≅ Bⁿ imply A ≅ B (in characteristic 0)?
85. **Köthe Conjecture** — the sum of two nil left ideals is nil.
86. **Nakai Conjecture** — smoothness detected by differential operators.
87. **Crouzeix's Conjecture** — a spectral-norm bound involving the numerical range (constant 2).

### Analysis, PDE & Operator Theory
88. **Navier–Stokes Existence and Smoothness** `★M` — do smooth solutions always exist globally in 3D?
89. **Invariant Subspace Problem** — does every bounded operator on separable Hilbert space have a non-trivial invariant subspace?
90. **Hilbert's Sixteenth Problem** — bounding limit cycles of planar polynomial vector fields.
91. **Schanuel's Conjecture** — a sweeping transcendence statement about exp.
92. **Four Exponentials Conjecture** — a special case of transcendence for 2×2 exponent grids.
93. **Sendov's Conjecture** — critical points near roots of complex polynomials (proven for large degree, open in general).

### Mathematical Physics & Theoretical Computer Science
94. **Yang–Mills Existence and Mass Gap** `★M` — rigorous quantum Yang–Mills theory with a positive mass gap.
95. **P versus NP** `★M` — can every efficiently-checkable problem be efficiently solved?
96. **Unique Games Conjecture** — hardness of approximation threshold.
97. **Exponential Time Hypothesis** — 3-SAT has no sub-exponential algorithm.

### Algebraic Geometry & Real Algebra
98. **Hodge Conjecture** `★M` — Hodge classes are combinations of algebraic cycles.
99. **Pierce–Birkhoff Conjecture** — piecewise-polynomial functions are max/min combinations of polynomials.
100. **Casas-Alvero Conjecture** — a polynomial sharing a root with each derivative is a pure power.

## Repository structure

```
problems/      One Markdown file per problem: statement, history, state of the art, references.
approaches/    Community-submitted approaches, ideas, and partial results.
```

Detailed problem pages are being filled in — flagship problems first. **Writing a missing `problems/NNN-*.md` page is one of the easiest ways to contribute.** Use [`problems/_TEMPLATE.md`](problems/_TEMPLATE.md).

## Contributing

Read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). In short:
- **Fix / expand a problem page** → PR.
- **Propose an approach** → add a file under `approaches/` following the template, or open an issue.
- **Report a problem as solved** → PR with a peer-reviewed citation.

Be rigorous, cite sources, and separate *proven* from *conjectured*. Good-faith partial ideas are welcome; grandiose unverifiable "proofs" will be closed with kindness.
